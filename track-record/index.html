The hardware got blamed. The testing found the bug.

Verifone's largest customer in Japan reported high card-read failures on a new terminal and blamed the industrial design: the angled sidewall of the swipe track. Retooling was on the table.

The blame

The client believed the sidewall shape made people misalign their cards. Tooling, recertification, and the Japan launch were all at risk.

Three weeks, no budget

A tightly scoped study in San Diego at two counter heights, with marked cards and every swipe reviewed on video.

Then Tokyo

A repeatable protocol run with a local research partner, comparing three terminals in randomized order.

The real cause

Failures tracked with slow card pulls, not the sidewall. The newer terminals shared a code base, so the evidence pointed to software.

Evidence over assumptions

The working assumption, at the client and inside Verifone, was that the hardware was at fault, and a software cause seemed unlikely. The product team held that it wasn't the hardware, and the data backed them up. Once engineering had the evidence, they traced the cause to software within two weeks and shipped a fix.

About $3M saved in tooling, recertification, and launch delay. The terminal shipped in Japan.
